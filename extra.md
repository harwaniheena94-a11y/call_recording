# Chapter Four

### The Sponsor

The next morning, I woke up with exactly one thought in my mind.

I needed coffee.

Not breakfast.

Not conversation.

Not human interaction.

Coffee.

I dragged myself out of bed, took a quick shower, threw on a pair of black trousers and a white shirt, and left my hair slightly messy because I couldn't be bothered to do anything about it.

Today was supposed to be simple.

A meeting.

A few signatures.

A sponsorship discussion.

Then I could go back to my peaceful existence where nobody bothered me.

At least, that was the plan.

I had no idea that my morning was about to become a personal attack from the universe.

---

“Viv, we're going to be late!”

I looked at Chloe through the mirror while fixing my hair.

“It's nine in the morning. Nobody should be awake at this hour.”

Chloe rolled her eyes.

“You're a Formula One driver.”

“Exactly. I drive cars at ridiculous speeds for a living. I don't wake up early.”

“You have a sponsorship meeting.”

“I know.”

“You've known for three days.”

“I know.”

“And you still decided to sleep until twenty minutes before we had to leave?”

I smiled.

“Yes.”

Chloe stared at me.

I smiled wider.

“I'm consistent.”

“You're impossible.”

“I prefer unpredictable.”

She grabbed my handbag from the bed and threw it at me.

“Move.”

I caught it.

“Violence against athletes. I'm reporting you.”

“Get in the car.”

“Yes, ma'am.”

---

The headquarters of the company sponsoring my racing team was enormous.

Glass.

Steel.

People wearing expensive suits.

The kind of building that silently screamed *I have more money than you.*

I looked up at it.

“Wow.”

Chloe glanced at me.

“Impressed?”

“Not really.”

“You're staring.”

“I'm judging.”

“Of course you are.”

We walked inside.

A receptionist greeted us immediately.

“Good morning. Ms. Vivienne?”

I nodded.

“Yes.”

“Mr. Lane is expecting you.”

I stopped walking.

“Mr. who?”

“Mr. Lane.”

Something about the name made my stomach twist.

Probably nothing.

There were millions of people in the world with the surname Lane.

Millions.

Surely the universe wasn't cruel enough to—

“Viv.”

Chloe's voice interrupted my thoughts.

“Come on.”

I followed her toward the elevators.

But something felt wrong.

Very wrong.

---

The elevator doors opened.

We stepped into a large conference room.

Several executives were already seated around the table.

I recognized some of them from previous sponsorship meetings.

Then I saw him.

I stopped.

For one second, I genuinely forgot how to breathe.

Black suit.

Black shirt.

Dark hair.

That same emotionless face.

Those same dark blue eyes.

The man from the club.

The man who had been sitting across from me last night.

The man whose friend had given me a pen.

The man I had decided was probably some kind of human-shaped warning sign.

Asher.

No.

Not Asher.

Someone else was sitting at the head of the table.

And the moment I looked at him, I knew.

My stomach dropped.

It was him.

The man from the club who had watched me from across the room.

The man whose presence had made the air feel heavier.

Arthur Lane.

He was wearing a perfectly tailored black suit, his expression as unreadable as ever.

His eyes met mine.

And for the first time since I had entered the room, his expression changed.

Barely.

A flicker of recognition.

Then nothing.

“Ms. Vivienne.”

His voice was deep.

Calm.

Controlled.

I swallowed.

“Mr. Lane.”

Chloe looked between us.

Her eyebrows slowly rose.

I could practically hear the question forming in her head.

*You know him?*

I ignored her.

Arthur gestured toward the empty chair.

“Please, sit.”

I sat.

Chloe took the seat beside me.

I kept my eyes on the documents in front of me.

This was fine.

Completely fine.

I could do this.

He was just a businessman.

A very intimidating businessman.

A very handsome businessman.

A very—

Stop.

I mentally slapped myself.

Focus.

The meeting began.

One of the executives started discussing the upcoming racing season, promotional obligations, media appearances, brand campaigns, and sponsorship terms.

I listened.

Or tried to.

Because every few minutes, I could feel Arthur's eyes on me.

I refused to look at him.

Eventually, the executive slid a document toward me.

“Mr. Lane would like to discuss the renewal of your sponsorship agreement.”

I looked down at the contract.

“Renewal?”

“Yes.”

I frowned.

“I thought we already finalized the sponsorship for this season.”

“We did.”

I looked up.

Arthur was watching me.

“The company intends to increase its investment.”

I blinked.

“Increase?”

“Yes.”

“How much?”

He stated the figure.

I nearly choked.

I looked at Chloe.

She looked equally shocked.

I slowly turned back toward him.

“Why?”

Arthur leaned back in his chair.

“Because you're worth it.”

Silence.

I stared at him.

He stared back.

Something about the way he said it made my heartbeat stumble.

Not because of the money.

Because his voice sounded like he wasn't talking about the sponsorship.

I quickly looked back at the contract.

“That's a lot of money.”

“It is.”

“And what exactly do you want in return?”

“Results.”

I raised an eyebrow.

“That's it?”

“Among other things.”

There it was.

I knew there had to be something.

“What other things?”

Arthur pushed a document toward me.

“Appearances. Promotional events. Interviews. Company campaigns.”

I groaned.

“No.”

Chloe kicked my ankle under the table.

I ignored her.

Arthur's eyebrow lifted.

“No?”

“I don't do promotional campaigns.”

“You do if you want the sponsorship.”

I looked at him.

He looked at me.

Neither of us blinked.

I finally leaned back.

“You're annoying.”

A few people around the table suddenly became very interested in their documents.

Chloe covered her mouth.

Arthur, however, didn't react.

“You've known me for less than twenty-four hours.”

“That's enough.”

Something almost resembling amusement appeared in his eyes.

“Interesting.”

I narrowed mine.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

I hated that word.

*Nothing.*

People always said “nothing” when there was clearly something.

The meeting continued.

Eventually, the executives left the room, leaving only Arthur, Asher, Chloe, and me.

I looked around.

“Why are they leaving?”

Arthur closed the folder in front of him.

“Because the remaining discussion concerns you.”

I pointed toward Chloe.

“She stays.”

Chloe immediately nodded.

“Obviously.”

Arthur looked at her for a moment.

Then at me.

“Fine.”

I crossed my arms.

“What exactly do you want?”

Arthur stood.

He walked toward the large windows overlooking the city.

“Your team is restructuring its sponsorship and management agreements.”

“I know.”

“My company will be your primary sponsor.”

“I know.”

“And from this season onward, I'll personally oversee the sponsorship.”

I stared at him.

“You?”

“Yes.”

I laughed.

Actually laughed.

“You've got to be kidding me.”

Arthur turned around.

“I don't joke about business.”

I stopped laughing.

Oh.

He was serious.

“You're telling me that you're going to be personally involved with my racing career?”

“Not your racing career.”

He paused.

“Your sponsorship.”

“That's basically the same thing.”

“No.”

“Close enough.”

His eyes narrowed slightly.

“You argue a lot.”

“I've been told.”

“And you don't listen.”

“Also been told.”

“And yet you expect me to invest millions in you.”

I smiled.

“Apparently, you like bad decisions.”

For the first time, I saw something in his expression.

A tiny twitch at the corner of his mouth.

Not quite a smile.

But close.

Then he looked at me in that same unnerving way he'd looked at me the night before.

Like he was trying to figure me out.

I suddenly became aware of how quiet the room was.

“Why me?” I asked.

His expression became serious again.

“Because you're good.”

I blinked.

“That's it?”

“You're one of the best drivers I've seen.”

That caught me off guard.

He continued.

“You take risks other drivers wouldn't.”

“That's called being fearless.”

“No.”

His gaze held mine.

“That's called knowing exactly how much risk you can survive.”

I didn't have an answer for that.

Arthur walked back toward the table.

“You don't need someone to teach you how to drive.”

He stopped in front of me.

“You need someone who knows how to make sure the world pays attention when you win.”

For once, I was silent.

Chloe looked at me.

I looked at Arthur.

There was something about him that irritated me.

Something that fascinated me.

Something I couldn't explain.

And I hated it.

I picked up the contract.

“Fine.”

Arthur nodded.

“But,” I added, pointing at him, “don't expect me to become some obedient little sponsored princess.”

His eyes dropped briefly to the tiara-shaped hair accessory I'd forgotten was still sitting in my bag.

Then back to me.

“I wouldn't expect that from you.”

I smiled.

“Good.”

I stood.

“Because I'm difficult.”

Arthur's gaze remained on me.

“I know.”

The way he said those two words sent an unfamiliar shiver down my spine.

I ignored it.

We shook hands.

His palm was warm.

His grip firm.

And for one strange second, neither of us let go.

His eyes met mine.

Dark blue.

Unreadable.

Familiar.

Dangerous.

Then he released my hand.

“Welcome to Lane Enterprises, Vivienne.”

I smiled.

“God help you.”

Behind me, Chloe muttered,

“God help *both* of you.”

I turned toward her.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

I rolled my eyes.

I hated that word.

But as we walked out of the conference room, one thought refused to leave my mind.

The man from the club wasn't just some mysterious stranger.

He was my sponsor.

And apparently, from now on...

I was going to have to see him a lot.

This was going to be a disaster.
