# Sales Call Knowledge Report

## Customer Overview
- **Customer Name**: Brian Behrmann
- **Location**: 9A Wells Court, Noranda, WA 6062
- **Products Discussed**: 12 and 17-panel solar systems (475W panels), 5 kW Fox ESS hybrid inverter, 14 kW Fox ESS battery, grid inverter, and Plenty finance.
- **Call Outcome**: Martin emailed three quotes. Brian will review them and requested a follow-up in three weeks.

## Call Summary
Brian contacted Solar Junction to upgrade his old 12-panel solar system because his household grew from two to six people. Martin verified the home has single-phase power and advised against adding a battery to the old, low-output panels. He emailed Brian three quotes: a 12-panel replacement ($3,995), a 17-panel layout, and a 17-panel system with a 14 kW battery ($8,740 after rebates). Brian expressed budget concerns but was interested in interest-free government finance through Plenty. The call ended with Brian requesting a follow-up call in three weeks due to an ongoing separate project.

## Customer Questions
- **Q**: Is it cheaper to extend panels, put new ones, or add a battery?  
  **A**: Adding a battery to old panels is ineffective due to low power output. Martin recommended replacing them entirely with new 475W panels.
- **Q**: What does the hybrid inverter do?  
  **A**: It manages and stores daytime solar energy in a battery for nighttime use, avoiding grid draw.
- **Q**: Why isn't a 10 kW battery sufficient?  
  **A**: A 10 kW battery won't cover overnight usage for six people; a 14 kW battery ensures the household does not pull grid power before morning.
- **Q**: What happens to the old equipment?  
  **A**: Installers leave removed panels on-site for customer disposal, or can dispose of them for an additional $200 fee.
- **Q**: What are the interest-free loan repayments?  
  **A**: For the $8,740 system (minus a $500 deposit), a 96-month term through Plenty is $85.83 per month.

## Customer Concerns
- Budget cap of approximately $7,000.
- Panel compatibility and physical spacing on the roof.
- Shading issues and lack of morning sun on the front/east side of the property.
- The hassle and potential cost of disposing of decommissioned equipment.

## Products & Features Discussed
- 475W solar panels (12 and 17-panel configurations)
- 5 kW Fox ESS hybrid inverter and standard grid inverter
- 14 kW Fox ESS battery
- Government STC rebate and $1,300 WA battery rebate
- Plenty interest-free finance (terms up to 10 years)

## Key Information Shared
- Old 2.4 kW systems generate under 10 units daily, which is insufficient to charge modern batteries.
- Single-phase power was confirmed via the single main switch in the customer's meter box.
- Upgrading to a battery later requires discarding a grid inverter for a hybrid inverter, resulting in redundant costs.

## Sales Tips Learned
- Walk customers through checking their meter box live to quickly confirm technical specs like phase type.
- Share personal stories of purchasing solar to lower resistance to high pricing and build trust.
- Adapt panel layouts dynamically based on customer feedback regarding local shading and sun angles.

## Potential FAQ
- **Q**: Can I reuse my old mounting brackets?  
  **A**: No, new brackets are required for warranty compliance and to fit larger, modern panel dimensions.
- **Q**: Can you install panels near the roof's apex?  
  **A**: No, drilling near the core risks permanent leaks and violates Clean Energy Council guidelines.
- **Q**: Is there a deposit required?  
  **A**: Yes, a $500 initial deposit is required to start the project.
